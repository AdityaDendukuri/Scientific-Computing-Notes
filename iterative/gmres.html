26  The Generalized Minimal Residual (GMRES) Method

When a linear system \(\bA\bx = \bb\) is nonsymmetric or indefinite, the quadratic energy minimization framework of the Conjugate Gradient method ceases to apply. The Faber–Manteuffel theorem characterizes the exceptional matrix classes that admit an optimal Krylov method with a fixed short recurrence. No such recurrence gives optimal residuals for arbitrary nonsymmetric matrices, so GMRES retains the full Arnoldi basis.

The Generalized Minimal Residual (GMRES) method of Saad and Schultz chooses, at every step \(k\), a vector \(\bx^{(k)}\) in the affine Krylov subspace \(\bx^{(0)} + \mathcal{K}_k(\bA, \br^{(0)})\) that minimizes the Euclidean residual norm \(\|\bb - \bA\bx\|_2\) (Saad and Schultz 1986). GMRES constructs an orthonormal basis for the growing Krylov subspace by the Arnoldi process of the referenced section (Arnoldi 1951) and reduces the original problem to a small upper-Hessenberg least-squares problem. The minimizing residual is unique, and the iterate is unique when the reduced least-squares problem has full column rank.

26.1 Reduction to Hessenberg Least Squares

Let \(\br^{(0)}=\bb-\bA\bx^{(0)}\) be nonzero and let \(\beta=\|\br^{(0)}\|_2\). We run the Arnoldi iteration of the referenced result from \(\bv_1=\br^{(0)}/\beta\). By the referenced result, after \(k\) steps the columns of \(\bV_k\) form an orthonormal basis of \(\mathcal{K}_k(\bA,\br^{(0)})\) and \(\bA\bV_k=\bV_{k+1}\bar{\bH}_k\). These two facts reduce the residual minimization in \(\fR^n\) to a small least-squares problem.

NoteTheorem: The GMRES Projected Least-Squares Problem

Let any vector in the affine space \(\bx^{(0)} + \mathcal{K}_k(\bA, \br^{(0)})\) be parameterized as \(\bx = \bx^{(0)} + \bV_k \by\) for some vector \(\by \in \fR^k\). The residual vector satisfies \[ \begin{align} \br(\bx) = \bb - \bA(\bx^{(0)} + \bV_k \by) = \br^{(0)} - \bA \bV_k \by = \beta \bv_1 - \bV_{k+1} \bar{\bH}_k \by = \bV_{k+1} (\beta \be_1 - \bar{\bH}_k \by). \end{align} \] Because the columns of \(\bV_{k+1}\) are orthonormal, \(\bV_{k+1}\) is an isometry in the Euclidean norm: \[ \begin{align} \|\bb - \bA\bx\|_2 = \|\beta \be_1 - \bar{\bH}_k \by\|_2, \end{align} \] where \(\be_1 = (1, 0, ..., 0)^T \in \fR^{k+1}\). The GMRES iterate is \(\bx^{(k)} = \bx^{(0)} + \bV_k \by_k\), where \(\by_k\) uniquely solves the \((k+1) \times k\) upper Hessenberg least-squares problem \[ \begin{align} \by_k = \arg\min_{\by \in \fR^k} \|\beta \be_1 - \bar{\bH}_k \by\|_2. \end{align} \]

TipProof

Express any \(\bx \in \bx^{(0)} + \mathcal{K}_k(\bA, \br^{(0)})\) as \(\bx = \bx^{(0)} + \bV_k \by\) for \(\by \in \fR^k\). The residual vector is \[ \begin{align} \br(\bx) &= \bb - \bA(\bx^{(0)} + \bV_k \by) \\ &= (\bb - \bA\bx^{(0)}) - \bA \bV_k \by \\ &= \br^{(0)} - \bV_{k+1} \bar{\bH}_k \by. \end{align} \] Since \(\bv_1 = \br^{(0)} / \beta\), we have \(\br^{(0)} = \beta \bv_1 = \bV_{k+1}(\beta \be_1)\), where \(\be_1 = (1, 0, ..., 0)^T \in \fR^{k+1}\). Factoring \(\bV_{k+1}\) gives \[ \begin{align} \br(\bx) = \bV_{k+1} (\beta \be_1 - \bar{\bH}_k \by). \end{align} \] Because the columns of \(\bV_{k+1}\) are orthonormal, \(\bV_{k+1}^T \bV_{k+1} = \bI_{k+1}\), and the Euclidean norm satisfies \[ \begin{align} \|\br(\bx)\|_2^2 &= (\beta \be_1 - \bar{\bH}_k \by)^T \bV_{k+1}^T \bV_{k+1} (\beta \be_1 - \bar{\bH}_k \by) \\ &= \|\beta \be_1 - \bar{\bH}_k \by\|_2^2. \end{align} \] Thus \(\|\br(\bx)\|_2 = \|\beta \be_1 - \bar{\bH}_k \by\|_2\), reducing the \(n\)-dimensional optimization to the stated \((k+1) \times k\) Hessenberg least-squares problem.

NoteTheorem: Polynomial Residual Characterization and Spectral Bound

The \(k\)-th GMRES residual satisfies the polynomial minimization property \[ \begin{align} \|\br^{(k)}\|_2 = \min_{p \in \mathcal{P}_k,\, p(0)=1} \|p(\bA)\br^{(0)}\|_2. \end{align} \] If \(\bA\) is diagonalizable with \(\bA = \bX \boldsymbol{\Lambda} \bX^{-1}\), where \(\boldsymbol{\Lambda} = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1, ..., \lambda_n)\), then the relative residual is bounded by \[ \begin{align} \frac{\|\br^{(k)}\|_2}{\|\br^{(0)}\|_2} \leq \kappa_2(\bX) \min_{p \in \mathcal{P}_k,\, p(0)=1} \max_{\lambda \in \sigma(\bA)} |p(\lambda)|. \end{align} \]

TipProof

Any vector \(\bx \in \bx^{(0)} + \mathcal{K}_k(\bA, \br^{(0)})\) takes the form \(\bx = \bx^{(0)} + q(\bA)\br^{(0)}\) for some polynomial \(q \in \mathcal{P}_{k-1}\). The resulting residual is \[ \begin{align} \br(\bx) = \bb - \bA\bx = \br^{(0)} - \bA q(\bA)\br^{(0)} = (\bI - \bA q(\bA))\br^{(0)}. \end{align} \] Define \(p(z) = 1 - z q(z)\). Then \(p \in \mathcal{P}_k\) and \(p(0) = 1\). Conversely, any polynomial \(p \in \mathcal{P}_k\) satisfying \(p(0) = 1\) can be written as \(p(z) = 1 - z q(z)\) for some \(q \in \mathcal{P}_{k-1}\). Because GMRES minimizes \(\|\br(\bx)\|_2\) over all such vectors, the first equality holds.

If \(\bA = \bX \boldsymbol{\Lambda} \bX^{-1}\), then \(p(\bA) = \bX p(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}) \bX^{-1}\). Taking norms gives \[ \begin{align} \|p(\bA)\br^{(0)}\|_2 \leq \|\bX\|_2 \|p(\boldsymbol{\Lambda})\|_2 \|\bX^{-1}\|_2 \|\br^{(0)}\|_2 = \kappa_2(\bX) \left(\max_{\lambda \in \sigma(\bA)} |p(\lambda)|\right) \|\br^{(0)}\|_2, \end{align} \] which yields the spectral bound upon taking the minimum over all normalized polynomials \(p \in \mathcal{P}_k\).

26.2 Solution via Givens Rotations

NoteTheorem: Incremental QR Factorization of the Hessenberg Matrix

At step \(k\), applying the previously accumulated Givens rotations \(\bG_1, ..., \bG_{k-1}\) transforms the first \(k-1\) entries of the new column of \(\bar{\bH}_k\). The subdiagonal entry at position \((k+1, k)\) is then eliminated using a new Givens rotation \(\bG_k\) defined by \[ \begin{align} \bG_k = \begin{pmatrix} c_k & s_k \\ -s_k & c_k \end{pmatrix}, \qquad c_k = \frac{h_{kk}^{(k-1)}}{\sqrt{(h_{kk}^{(k-1)})^2 + h_{k+1,k}^2}}, \quad s_k = \frac{h_{k+1,k}}{\sqrt{(h_{kk}^{(k-1)})^2 + h_{k+1,k}^2}}. \end{align} \] Applying \(\bG_k ... \bG_1\) transforms \(\bar{\bH}_k\) into an upper triangular matrix \(\bR_k\) with a zero final row, and transforms the right-hand side vector \(\beta \be_1\) into \(\bg_{k+1} = (\gamma_1, ..., \gamma_k, \gamma_{k+1})^T\). The residual norm at iteration \(k\) is obtained without forming \(\bx^{(k)}\): \[ \begin{align} \|\br^{(k)}\|_2 = |\gamma_{k+1}|. \end{align} \]

TipProof

Let \(\bQ_k = \bG_k ... \bG_1 \in \fR^{(k+1) \times (k+1)}\) denote the product of Givens rotations. Because each Givens rotation is an orthogonal transformation on \(\fR^{k+1}\), \(\bQ_k\) is orthogonal. The least-squares problem satisfies \[ \begin{align} \min_{\by \in \fR^k} \|\beta \be_1 - \bar{\bH}_k \by\|_2^2 = \min_{\by \in \fR^k} \|\bQ_k(\beta \be_1 - \bar{\bH}_k \by)\|_2^2 = \min_{\by \in \fR^k} \left\| \begin{pmatrix} \gamma_{1:k} \\ \gamma_{k+1} \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} \bR_k \\ \bzero^T \end{pmatrix} \by \right\|_2^2. \end{align} \] Expanding the squared Euclidean norm: \[ \begin{align} \min_{\by \in \fR^k} \left( \|\gamma_{1:k} - \bR_k \by\|_2^2 + |\gamma_{k+1}|^2 \right). \end{align} \] Because \(\bR_k \in \fR^{k \times k}\) is upper triangular and nonsingular (assuming no earlier breakdown), we can choose \(\by_k = \bR_k^{-1} \gamma_{1:k}\) to make the first term identically zero. The remaining squared residual is exactly \(|\gamma_{k+1}|^2\), proving that \(\|\br^{(k)}\|_2 = |\gamma_{k+1}|\).

TipRemark

(Happy breakdown vs. stagnation) If the subdiagonal entry \(h_{k+1,k}\) vanishes, the referenced result shows that the Krylov subspace is invariant under \(\bA\). When \(\bA\) is nonsingular, its restriction to this invariant subspace is nonsingular, so the GMRES least-squares problem has residual zero and \(\bx^{(k)}\) is the exact solution. Termination for this reason is called happy breakdown. Stagnation is a different phenomenon. The Krylov space may continue to grow while its image under \(\bA\) contributes no useful correction to the residual. Non-normality, an unfavorable polynomial approximation problem, or a poor preconditioner can all cause this behavior, and the residual history alone does not identify which one is responsible.

26.3 Restarted GMRES and Practical Considerations

NoteDefinition: Restarted GMRES(\(m\))

Full GMRES requires storing \(k\) basis vectors of length \(n\), incurring \(O(kn)\) memory storage and \(O(k^2 n)\) orthogonalization flops. To avoid memory exhaustion on large systems, the algorithm is restarted every \(m\) steps:

  1. Run \(m\) steps of GMRES starting from \(\bx^{(0)}\) to produce \(\bx^{(m)}\).

  2. Compute the residual \(\br^{(m)} = \bb - \bA\bx^{(m)}\).

  3. Clear the Krylov basis vectors, set \(\bx^{(0)} \leftarrow \bx^{(m)}\), and repeat.

TipRemark

(The trade-off of restarting) Restarted GMRES(\(m\)) bounds memory usage to \(O(mn)\) and bounds orthogonalization cost per cycle. However, restarting discards the accumulated Krylov subspace and destroys the global optimality property. If \(m\) is chosen too small, the algorithm may stagnate completely.

26.4 Exercises

WarningExercise

  1. Compute. (Arnoldi factorization by hand) For the matrix and initial residual \[ \begin{align} \bA = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \br^{(0)} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}, \end{align} \] perform two steps of the Arnoldi iteration by hand.

    1. Compute \(\bv_1\) and \(\bw_1 = \bA\bv_1\). Find \(h_{11}, h_{21}\) and \(\bv_2\).

    2. Compute \(\bw_2 = \bA\bv_2\). Find \(h_{12}, h_{22}, h_{32}\) and \(\bv_3\).

    3. Explicitly assemble the matrices \(\bV_2\) and the \(3 \times 2\) upper Hessenberg matrix \(\bar{\bH}_2\).

  2. Compute. (Hessenberg QR and residual norm via Givens) Using the \(3 \times 2\) matrix \(\bar{\bH}_2\) from Exercise 1:

    1. Form the Givens rotation \(\bG_1\) that eliminates \(h_{21}\), and the rotation \(\bG_2\) that eliminates \(h_{32}\).

    2. Apply these rotations to the right-hand side vector \(\beta \be_1 = (\sqrt{2}, 0, 0)^T\).

    3. Read off the residual norm \(\|\br^{(2)}\|_2\) from the transformed vector without computing \(\bx^{(2)}\).

  3. Derive. (GMRES polynomial minimax on an ellipse) Suppose the eigenvalues of \(\bA\) are enclosed within an ellipse in the complex plane centered at \(c > 0\) with focal distance \(d < c\) that does not contain the origin.

    1. Using the scaled Chebyshev polynomial \(p(z) = T_k((c - z)/d) / T_k(c/d)\), show that \(p(0) = 1\).

    2. Apply the referenced result to derive an explicit upper bound for \(\|\br^{(k)}\|_2 / \|\br^{(0)}\|_2\) in terms of the semi-axes of the ellipse.

  4. Explore. (Exact stagnation for a cyclic permutation) Let \(\bA\) be the cyclic permutation matrix defined by \(\bA\be_j=\be_{j+1}\) for \(j<n\) and \(\bA\be_n=\be_1\). Take \(\bx^{(0)}=\bzero\) and \(\bb=\be_1\).

    1. Prove that \(\mathcal{K}_k(\bA,\bb)=\operatorname{span}\{\be_1,...,\be_k\}\) for \(k<n\).

    2. Show that \(\bA\mathcal{K}_k=\operatorname{span}\{\be_2,...,\be_{k+1}\}\) is orthogonal to \(\bb\). Conclude that \(\|\br^{(k)}\|_2=1\) for every \(k<n\).

    3. Show that \(\bx=\be_n\) becomes available at step \(n\) and gives zero residual. Construct the degree-\(n\) residual polynomial that vanishes on the spectrum of \(\bA\), and explain why no polynomial of smaller degree can do so.

  5. Explore. (Left versus right preconditioning) Compare left preconditioning (\(\bM^{-1}\bA\bx = \bM^{-1}\bb\)) and right preconditioning (\(\bA\bM^{-1}\by = \bb\), \(\bx = \bM^{-1}\by\)). Show that right-preconditioned GMRES minimizes the true residual \(\|\bb-\bA\bx\|_2\), whereas left-preconditioned GMRES minimizes \(\|\bM^{-1}(\bb-\bA\bx)\|_2\). Explain why a code that uses left preconditioning may need to monitor both quantities.
