16  Krylov Subspaces and the Arnoldi Process

The QR algorithm of the referenced result computes every eigenvalue of a dense matrix in \(O(n^3)\) operations. Many applications instead require a few eigenvalues of a large sparse matrix, and the matrix is often available only through the product \(\bv\mapsto\bA\bv\). Power iteration (the referenced result) uses only such products, but at step \(k\) it retains only the last vector \(\bA^{k-1}\bv\) and discards the earlier ones.

The Arnoldi process keeps the entire sequence \(\bv,\bA\bv,...,\bA^{k-1}\bv\). We first define the subspace spanned by this sequence and explain why its natural basis is unusable in floating-point arithmetic. We then construct an orthonormal basis one vector at a time and derive the Arnoldi relation. The same relation underlies the Lanczos algorithm in the next section, the GMRES method for linear systems, and the Krylov approximation of the matrix exponential.

16.1 From Power Iteration to Krylov Subspaces

NoteDefinition: Krylov Subspace

Let \(\bA\in\fR^{n\times n}\) and let \(\bv\in\fR^n\) be nonzero. The \(k\)th Krylov subspace generated by \(\bA\) and \(\bv\) is \[ \begin{align} \mathcal{K}_k(\bA,\bv)=\operatorname{span}\{\bv,\bA\bv,...,\bA^{k-1}\bv\}. \end{align} \] Equivalently, \(\mathcal{K}_k(\bA,\bv)=\{p(\bA)\bv : p\in\mathcal{P}_{k-1}\}\), where \(\mathcal{P}_{k-1}\) denotes the polynomials of degree at most \(k-1\).

The \(k\)th power iterate is a multiple of \(\bA^{k-1}\bv\), so it lies in \(\mathcal{K}_k(\bA,\bv)\). Power iteration therefore selects one particular vector from the Krylov subspace, namely the one produced by the polynomial \(p(t)=t^{k-1}\). Any method that searches the whole subspace can do at least as well with the same number of matrix–vector products. the referenced section makes this comparison precise for symmetric matrices.

NoteLemma: Krylov Subspaces Stop Growing at an Invariant Subspace

If \(\mathcal{K}_{k+1}(\bA,\bv)=\mathcal{K}_k(\bA,\bv)\), then \(\mathcal{K}_k(\bA,\bv)\) is invariant under \(\bA\) in the sense of the referenced result, and \(\mathcal{K}_j(\bA,\bv)=\mathcal{K}_k(\bA,\bv)\) for every \(j\geq k\).

TipProof

Every vector in \(\mathcal{K}_k\) has the form \(p(\bA)\bv\) with \(\deg p\leq k-1\). Multiplying by \(\bA\) gives \(\bA p(\bA)\bv\), which is a polynomial of degree at most \(k\) in \(\bA\) applied to \(\bv\), and therefore lies in \(\mathcal{K}_{k+1}\). Hence \(\bA\mathcal{K}_k\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{k+1}=\mathcal{K}_k\), which is the invariance claim. Since \(\mathcal{K}_{k+2}=\mathcal{K}_{k+1}+\bA\mathcal{K}_{k+1}=\mathcal{K}_k+\bA\mathcal{K}_k=\mathcal{K}_k\), induction gives the second claim.

TipRemark

(Ill-conditioned monomial basis) The vectors \(\bv,\bA\bv,...,\bA^{k-1}\bv\) form a basis of \(\mathcal{K}_k\) whenever the subspace has dimension \(k\), but the basis is useless in practice. By the argument behind power iteration, the normalized vectors \(\bA^j\bv/\|\bA^j\bv\|_2\) converge to the dominant eigenvector, so the later columns of the basis matrix become nearly parallel. Its condition number grows geometrically with \(k\), and rounding errors destroy the information carried by the smaller components.

16.2 The Arnoldi Process

The remedy is to orthonormalize the Krylov sequence as it is generated. If \(\bv_1,...,\bv_j\) is an orthonormal basis of \(\mathcal{K}_j\), we obtain a new direction by multiplying the latest basis vector by \(\bA\) instead of forming \(\bA^j\bv\). We then remove its components along the existing basis by Gram–Schmidt orthogonalization (the referenced result).

NoteDefinition: The Arnoldi Iteration

Let \(\bA\in\fR^{n\times n}\) and let \(\bv\in\fR^n\) be nonzero. Set \(\bv_1=\bv/\|\bv\|_2\). For \(j=1,2,...,k\):

  1. Compute \(\bw=\bA\bv_j\).

  2. For \(i=1,...,j\), compute \(h_{ij}=\bv_i^T\bw\) and update \(\bw\leftarrow\bw-h_{ij}\bv_i\).

  3. Compute \(h_{j+1,j}=\|\bw\|_2\).

  4. If \(h_{j+1,j}=0\), stop. Otherwise, set \(\bv_{j+1}=\bw/h_{j+1,j}\).

Step 2 is modified Gram–Schmidt. The coefficients \(h_{ij}\) are collected in the \(k\times k\) upper Hessenberg matrix \(\bH_k\) and in the \((k+1)\times k\) matrix \(\bar{\bH}_k\), which is \(\bH_k\) with the additional row \(h_{k+1,k}\be_k^T\).

NoteTheorem: The Arnoldi Relation

Suppose the Arnoldi iteration completes \(k\) steps with \(h_{j+1,j}\neq0\) for \(j=1,...,k\), and let \(\bV_k=(\bv_1\ \cdots\ \bv_k)\). Then:

  1. The columns of \(\bV_k\) are orthonormal and span \(\mathcal{K}_k(\bA,\bv)\).

  2. The matrices satisfy $$ \[\begin{align} \bA\bV_k=\bV_k\bH_k+h_{k+1,k}\bv_{k+1}\be_k^T=\bV_{k+1}\bar{\bH}_k. \end{align}\] $$

  3. The Hessenberg matrix is the projection of \(\bA\) onto the Krylov subspace, \(\bH_k=\bV_k^T\bA\bV_k\).

TipProof

Step 2 of the iteration subtracts from \(\bw\) its components along \(\bv_1,...,\bv_j\) one at a time. After the subtraction, \(\bw\) is orthogonal to each of these vectors, and normalizing it gives a unit vector \(\bv_{j+1}\) orthogonal to \(\bv_1,...,\bv_j\). Induction on \(j\) proves orthonormality.

Rearranging the definition of \(\bv_{j+1}\) gives the column relation \[ \begin{align} \bA\bv_j=\sum_{i=1}^{j+1}h_{ij}\bv_i,\qquad j=1,...,k. \end{align} \] Placing these \(k\) columns side by side gives \(\bA\bV_k=\bV_{k+1}\bar{\bH}_k\). Separating the last row of \(\bar{\bH}_k\) gives the first form of the referenced equation.

For the span, assume \(\operatorname{span}\{\bv_1,...,\bv_j\}=\mathcal{K}_j\). The column relation expresses \(\bv_{j+1}\) as a combination of \(\bA\bv_j\) and \(\bv_1,...,\bv_j\), and \(\bA\bv_j\in\bA\mathcal{K}_j\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{j+1}\). Hence \(\bv_{j+1}\in\mathcal{K}_{j+1}\). The \(j+1\) orthonormal vectors \(\bv_1,...,\bv_{j+1}\) are linearly independent and lie in \(\mathcal{K}_{j+1}\), whose dimension is at most \(j+1\), so they span it.

Finally, multiplying the referenced equation on the left by \(\bV_k^T\) and using \(\bV_k^T\bV_k=\bI_k\) and \(\bV_k^T\bv_{k+1}=\bzero\) gives \(\bV_k^T\bA\bV_k=\bH_k\).

TipRemark

(Arnoldi cost) Step \(j\) requires one product with \(\bA\) and \(j\) inner products and vector updates of length \(n\). After \(k\) steps the iteration has used \(k\) matrix–vector products and \(O(k^2n)\) additional operations, and it stores \(k+1\) vectors of length \(n\). The orthogonalization cost and storage both grow with \(k\). For a symmetric matrix, the referenced section shows that most of the coefficients \(h_{ij}\) vanish and that the growth disappears.

16.3 Ritz Pairs and Breakdown

The matrix \(\bH_k\) is small, so its eigenvalues can be computed by the dense QR algorithm. Its eigenpairs, lifted back to \(\fR^n\) through \(\bV_k\), serve as approximate eigenpairs of \(\bA\). The Arnoldi relation measures their quality without any further products with \(\bA\).

NoteDefinition: Ritz Pairs

Let \(\bH_k\bs=\theta\bs\) with \(\|\bs\|_2=1\). The scalar \(\theta\) is a Ritz value and the unit vector \(\tilde{\bu}=\bV_k\bs\in\mathcal{K}_k(\bA,\bv)\) is the corresponding Ritz vector of \(\bA\) with respect to \(\mathcal{K}_k(\bA,\bv)\).

NoteTheorem: Residual of a Ritz Pair

Let \((\theta,\tilde{\bu})\) be a Ritz pair with \(\tilde{\bu}=\bV_k\bs\), and let \(s_k\) denote the last entry of \(\bs\). Then \[ \begin{align} \bA\tilde{\bu}-\theta\tilde{\bu}=h_{k+1,k}s_k\,\bv_{k+1}, \qquad \|\bA\tilde{\bu}-\theta\tilde{\bu}\|_2=h_{k+1,k}|s_k|. \end{align} \]

TipProof

Multiplying the referenced equation on the right by \(\bs\) gives \[ \begin{align} \bA\tilde{\bu} =\bV_k\bH_k\bs+h_{k+1,k}\bv_{k+1}(\be_k^T\bs) =\theta\tilde{\bu}+h_{k+1,k}s_k\bv_{k+1}. \end{align} \] Subtracting \(\theta\tilde{\bu}\) and using \(\|\bv_{k+1}\|_2=1\) gives both claims.

The residual norm depends only on \(h_{k+1,k}\) and the last entry of a \(k\)-dimensional eigenvector. We can therefore monitor convergence of every Ritz pair at each step and form \(\tilde{\bu}=\bV_k\bs\) only for the pairs that have converged. A small residual shows that \((\theta,\tilde{\bu})\) is an exact eigenpair of a nearby matrix. For a symmetric matrix, the referenced result then places an eigenvalue within the residual norm of \(\theta\). For a nonnormal diagonalizable matrix, the referenced result multiplies this distance by the condition number of the eigenvector basis, which can be large.

NoteCorollary: Breakdown Gives Exact Eigenvalues

If the Arnoldi iteration stops at step \(k\) because \(h_{k+1,k}=0\), then \(\mathcal{K}_k(\bA,\bv)\) is invariant under \(\bA\), and every Ritz pair is an exact eigenpair of \(\bA\).

TipProof

When \(h_{k+1,k}=0\), the referenced result gives \(\bA\tilde{\bu}=\theta\tilde{\bu}\) for every Ritz pair. The relation \(\bA\bV_k=\bV_k\bH_k\) also shows that \(\bA\) maps the column space of \(\bV_k\), which is \(\mathcal{K}_k\), into itself.

16.4 Exercises

WarningExercise

  1. Check. (Shift invariance) Show that \(\mathcal{K}_k(\bA-\sigma\bI,\bv)=\mathcal{K}_k(\bA,\bv)\) for every scalar \(\sigma\). Conclude that the Arnoldi iteration applied to \(\bA-\sigma\bI\) produces the same basis \(\bV_k\) and the Hessenberg matrix \(\bH_k-\sigma\bI\).

  2. Derive. (Exact termination) Suppose \(\bv\) is a combination of exactly \(m\) eigenvectors of a diagonalizable matrix \(\bA\), with nonzero coefficients and distinct eigenvalues. Prove that \(\dim\mathcal{K}_k(\bA,\bv)=\min(k,m)\), and use the referenced result and the referenced result to conclude that the Arnoldi iteration stops at step \(m\) with those \(m\) eigenvalues as its Ritz values.

  3. Compute. (Monomial versus Arnoldi basis) Let \(\bA=\operatorname{diag}(1,2,...,100)\) and \(\bv=(1,...,1)^T\). For \(k=5,10,15,20\), compute the condition number of the matrix with columns \(\bv,\bA\bv,...,\bA^{k-1}\bv\), and the quantity \(\|\bV_k^T\bV_k-\bI\|_2\) for the Arnoldi basis. Explain the growth of the first quantity using power iteration.

  4. Compute. (Implementing Arnoldi) Implement the referenced result for a function that applies \(\bA\) to a vector. Test it on a random \(200\times200\) nonsymmetric matrix by checking \(\|\bA\bV_k-\bV_{k+1}\bar{\bH}_k\|_2\) and by comparing the residuals of the referenced result with directly computed residuals \(\|\bA\tilde{\bu}-\theta\tilde{\bu}\|_2\).
